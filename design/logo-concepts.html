Three Marks for Trésor

Each concept proofed in both mandatory colorways — gold on charcoal, ink on cream — at the same optical size.

Concept A

The Vault Arch

A single unbroken line drawing the doorway of a private vault; a hairline echo gives the threshold depth. The gold sill is the line you are invited across.

Gold · Charcoal
Ink · Cream

Strengths: quietest of the three; scales to favicon; reads "entry, privacy, architecture."
Risk: the arch is a spatial idea, not a personal one — it says where, never whose. Archways are also common currency in boutique-hotel identities.

Concept B

The Ledger Monogram

T and E interlocked on one shared crossbar — the T's arm becomes the E's first ledger line — with the accent aigu keeping its gold stroke above.

Gold · Charcoal
Ink · Cream

Strengths: the ruled-line construction genuinely encodes the ledger idea; the gold aigu carries the wordmark's one accessory into the mark.
Risk: it competes with the existing italic-T monogram for the same job (app icon, avatar) rather than adding a new register.

Concept C

The Provenance Seal

A double hairline ring circumscribed in letterspaced small caps, holding the existing italic-T monogram and its gold aigu at the center — a stamp, not a badge.

Gold · Charcoal
Ink · Cream

Strengths: extends the identity instead of replacing it — the monogram we already own, given a ceremonial frame; born for provenance moments (ledger stamps, borrow certificates, watermarks).
Risk: the circumscription needs ≥64px to stay legible; below that, the ring drops away and the bare monogram takes over.

✦ Recommended

Recommendation

Concept C — The Provenance Seal

The brand guide is firm that the word is the treasure — Trésor's primary mark is the wordmark, and its monogram is the italic T. Concepts A and B are handsome, but each would be a second primary mark: the arch introduces a symbol the system never asked for, and the ledger monogram competes with the italic T for the app icon.

The seal does neither. It takes the monogram we already own and gives it a ceremonial register — the identity becomes a three-part system with defined roles: wordmark for headers and welcome, monogram for icon and avatars, seal for moments of provenance. Every place the product treats an object like a consignment — the borrow ledger, a completed return, a watermark on item photography, a printed circle invitation — is a place the seal gets stamped. That is the "Provenance Ledger" idea made visible, and it matches the wax-seal role circular marks play across current luxury identity systems (see design/research/design-inspiration-v3.md §4.1).

Both colorways hold: on charcoal the hairline rings sit at the same whisper volume as the ledger rules; on cream the ink seal reads like a notary stamp, with the aigu in gold-deep per the small-gold-on-light contrast rule. Below 64px the rings retire and the bare monogram continues — no new asset needed at favicon scale.
