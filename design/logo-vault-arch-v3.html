Identity · Round 3

The Vault Arch — Rebuilt

All three V1 variations were rejected. V1 drew lines about an arch; a luxury mark is a built object. These three are constructed — stone by stone, plane by plane, line by line — after studying the marks of Cartier, Hermès, Boucheron and the architecture of the Roman arch itself.

Cartier · 1900

One enclosed form

The interlaced-C monogram has held since 1900 because it is a single, perfectly proportioned form inside a containing oval. Restraint is earned by proportion — nothing floats, nothing is arbitrary.

Hermès · 1950

Detail, disciplined

The Duc attelé carriage is a genuinely detailed illustration — wheels, ironwork, groom — yet reads as one seal because every line serves the silhouette. Detail is not the enemy of luxury; looseness is.

Rome · The Arch

Radial truth

A real arch stands because every voussoir joint radiates from one center point, locked by the keystone. Draw the joints on true radials and the arch feels load-bearing; draw them casually and it feels like clip-art.

Boucheron · 1937

Built geometry

Boucheron's Deco tiaras built arches from interlocking geometric stones — arcs, steps, baguettes — never from a single drawn curve. The arch as marquetry: constructed planes, active negative space.

The V1 postmortem, honored throughout: no bare curve (V1-1, "too basic"), no stacked arcs with whisper fills (V1-2, "not good enough"), no letterform inside the arch (V1-3, "awful"). Every mark below must survive the test: does it look built, or drawn?

Three Registers

Stone · Inlay · Engraving

The same doorway rendered three ways: as masonry in elevation, as Art Deco gold marquetry, and as a hairline heraldic engraving. In each, the keystone carries the light — the one bright element, the É-accent principle translated into architecture.

A
Variation A

The Architectural Vault

A private treasury entrance in true architectural elevation — solid gold masonry with charcoal mortar. Nine stones form the arch: eight voussoirs on radial joints aimed at the arc's center, locked by a projecting keystone in bright gold — the only lit stone, the lock of the whole structure. Impost moldings carry the springing line; coursed jamb blocks frame the door; a plinth grounds it. Inside the opening, two receding steps and a reveal arch fall away into darkness — the vault interior, unlit, waiting. Roman triumphal arch meets bank vault: not a drawing of a door, but the door itself, cast in metal.

Ink · Cream — 128px & 32px

Construction. Center of the arc at (50,58) — the springing line. Extrados R=37, intrados r=26, keystone crown projecting to y=16.5. The ring is one filled half-annulus; mortar joints are ground-colored hairlines laid on true radials at 19.75°, 39.5° and 59.25° each side, so all nine stones aim at the center — the geometry that makes a real arch stand.
  • Keystone — trapezoid M41.5 16.5 L58.5 16.5 L54.8 32.5 L45.2 32.5, wider at top, projecting 4.5 units above the extrados. Always the bright stone: #E8D5A3 on charcoal, #9A7E4A on cream — the É-accent rule carried into masonry.
  • Springing line — impost moldings project 2.5 units past both the arch face and the jambs, the classic structural transition.
  • Jambs — three ashlar courses per side (joints at y=69.7, 77.8) on a plinth; the whole elevation sits on a hairline ground-shadow at y=92.5.
  • Depth — two step lines fading at 65% → 45% opacity and a 50%-opacity reveal arch (r=22.5) recede into the unlit opening. The interior is ground color: the vault is dark because it is closed.
  • At 32px — steps, reveal, jamb courses and four of six joints retire; the silhouette holds as arch + keystone + imposts + jambs + plinth. Mortar widens to 1.2–1.6 for optical weight.
B
Variation B

The Geometric Inlay

The doorway as Art Deco marquetry — a stepped portal built entirely from closed geometric planes in two golds, the way Boucheron built arches from interlocking stones. Three courses step outward as they descend; a half-disk crown in two concentric bands closes the top; a squared Deco keystone breaks the crown's silhouette. Through all of it runs the negative space: a slender arched aperture cut from base to crown — the same Trésor doorway, this time formed by absence. Hairline structure lines trace every plane like the engraved framework on a vault-door face. Bright gold and deep gold alternate course by course — lacquer and shadow, marquetry in metal.

Gold · Charcoal — 128px & 32px

Ink · Cream — 128px & 32px

Construction. Every arc shares one center, (50,58) — the same radial discipline as Variation A, translated into Deco. Crown bands at r=22→15 (bright) and r=15→9 (deep); the aperture void is the r=9 crown continued as an 18-unit slot to the base. Three courses step outward by 6 units per drop of 10 — the Deco ziggurat rhythm, no zigzags, no sunbursts.
  • Built, not drawn — nine closed filled elements and zero stroked curves. The arch shape emerges from the assembly, exactly what V1-2's stacked arcs failed to do.
  • Two tones only — #E8D5A3 and #9A7E4A alternating per course on charcoal; #1a1715 and #9A7E4A on cream. Fills are solid — marquetry, not a CSS gradient.
  • Deco keystone — an 8×10 squared insert at the crown, deep tone against the bright outer band, projecting 3 units above the silhouette. The one asymmetry of tone at the axis of symmetry.
  • Structure lines — every plane outlined at 0.6: #C9A961 on charcoal, #1a1715 on cream. They read as engraved framework at 128px.
  • Negative space is the doorway — the aperture (x=41→59, crown r=9) is never filled; the ground color passes through the entire mark.
  • At 32px — structure lines, keystone and ground line retire; the two-tone silhouette alone carries it, and the tones stay distinguishable.
C
Variation C

The Heraldic Crest

The arch as the charge on a house crest — hairline engraving in the register of Asprey's heritage crest and the Hermès seal. A heater shield with a double hairline border encloses the vault arch drawn small but complete: keystone, radial joints, imposts, jambs, threshold. Below, a ribbon banner with swallowtail ends carries the TRÉSOR wordmark in letterspaced Playfair — the ribbon chosen over laurel because Trésor's register is institutional, a private bank's engraving, not a classical wreath. No fills anywhere except the keystone, which alone is gold. Something to stamp into wax, engrave above a treasury door, or press into the cover of a ledger.

Ink · Cream — 128px & 32px

Construction. Heater shield 56 × 60.5 units, straight chief, sides drawn in single cubic curves to the point at (50,72.5); inner border inset 2.8 at 55% weight — the double hairline of an engraved bookplate. The charge is the vault arch at R=19 / r=13 from center (50,47), filling roughly 60% of the interior height and drawn with the same radial-joint discipline as Variation A.
  • One flourish only — the ribbon, not laurel. Band curved on a single quadratic (Q50 84 top, Q50 92 bottom), swallowtail tails notched at both ends, fold lines where they pass behind the band.
  • Wordmark — TRÉSOR in Playfair Display 500 at font-size 6.8, letterspaced 0.2em, set on a textPath along the ribbon's midline so the baseline follows the curve. Per §1.1 the accent is gold; at this scale the whole É carries it (#E8D5A3 dark / #9A7E4A cream) — at production sizes only the aigu itself is gold.
  • Hairlines throughout — 0.55 to 1.0, no fills, no gradients. The single exception is the keystone, filled gold: one lit stone inside an ink engraving, the crest's É-accent.
  • At 32px — ribbon, wordmark, joints and inner border retire. The shield thickens to 2.4 and stretches taller (y=8→76.5) to reclaim the ribbon's space; the arch becomes a solid filled band with jambs so it reads as architecture, not a curve. Keystone stays gold.
  • Role note — this register overlaps the Provenance Seal's ceremonial territory (§1.5); if chosen, it should replace the seal in ledger stamps and certificates, not sit beside it.

For Review

Where Each Mark Would Live

A · Architectural Vault

The hero candidate. Strongest silhouette of the three, fully self-sufficient at 32px, and the only one that reads as a physical object — a door you could close. Welcome screen, app icon ground, brand mark.

B · Geometric Inlay

The engraved surface. Two-tone marquetry wants flat, controlled grounds: vault-door detail in the app's dark screens, embossed packaging, loading states. Most modern of the three; most dependent on its two tones staying crisp.

C · Heraldic Crest

The ceremonial stamp. Borrow certificates, ledger stamps, printed circle invitations. Carries the wordmark within itself — but competes for the Provenance Seal's jobs, so choosing it is also a decision about §1.5.

My recommendation is Variation A. It answers every rejection directly: nine stones where V1 had one stroke, radial construction where V1 had casual curves, and a keystone that gives the mark a single point of light — the same discipline as the gold aigu in the wordmark. It is the only one of the three that would still feel inevitable carved above a real vault door. B and C are strong supporting registers if the system wants them later.
