Identity · Round 4

The Vault Arch — The Minimalist Middle

Round 1 was a line with no weight. Round 3 was architecture with too much. The direction is a little more minimalist — so these three scale back to one confident form and at most one accent, the way Tiffany, Cartier and Hermès earn luxury through restraint rather than ornament.

Round 1 · Rejected

Too basic — no mass

The single-line arch, the stacked arcs, the arch-and-T: all of them drew a doorway instead of making an object. Tentative strokes, whisper fills, nothing you could hold. The mark had no presence, so it had no luxury.

Round 3 · Rejected

Too detailed — no calm

Nine-stone masonry, Deco marquetry, a heraldic crest. Beautifully built and immediately busy — voussoir joints, impost moldings, ribbon banners. Every element justified, and still too many elements. Construction became clutter.

Round 4 · The Formula

One form, one accent

How the houses do it: Tiffany's box is a solid shape with one ribbon. Cartier's mark is two curves meeting in perfect proportion. Hermès frames the name and stops. Confident form, a single defining detail, and negative space doing the rest.

Every mark below must pass both prior tests at once: does it have mass? (the Round 1 failure) and could you remove anything? (the Round 3 failure). If the answer to the second question is yes, it is not done. Minimal is not empty; it is finished.

Variation 01 Tiffany register

The Solid Silhouette

The arch as a solid filled form — not a drawing of a doorway but the doorway itself, cut from one piece of metal. This is what Round 1 lacked: mass. Like the Tiffany box, the shape is simple and the presence is total. One detail only: a keystone inset at the crown, a small bright-gold trapezoid seated where the arch locks — the É-accent principle carried into the silhouette. An object, not a line. A single jewel, not a pattern.

  • The keystone inset — a trapezoid M45.4 28.5 L54.6 28.5 L52.6 37 L47.4 37 Z, wider at top like a true keystone, seated 2.5 units below the crown edge so it reads as an inlay, not a break. Always the bright stone: #E8D5A3 on charcoal, #9A7E4A on cream — one accent, per the É rule (§1.1).
  • Tonal discipline — bright-gold-on-gold keeps the inset quiet on dark grounds, a jewel catching light; on cream the deep gold sits legibly in the ink per the small-gold contrast rule (§2.1).
  • At 32px — the silhouette holds unchanged; the trapezoid simplifies to a diamond (M50 28 L54.8 34 L50 40 L45.2 34 Z), enlarged for optical weight. Below 24px the diamond may retire; the solid arch alone still reads.
  • Why it survives both postmortems — total mass (the Round 1 cure) from exactly two elements (the Round 3 cure).
Variation 02 Cartier register

The Converging Lines

Two lines rise from the base, curve inward, and meet at a single point — the keystone position, held by geometry alone. No fill, no separate keystone shape: the convergence is the keystone. The arch itself is pure negative space between the strokes, the way Cartier's two Cs make a form out of what is not there. The strokes are confident — 3.5 units, round caps — because the Round 1 lesson was never that lines are wrong, only that tentative lines are.

Gold · Charcoal — 128px & 32px

Ink · Cream — 128px & 32px

Construction. Two mirrored strokes sharing one arc center at (50,52), r=22. Left: M28 88 L28 52 A22 22 0 0 1 50 30. Right: M72 88 L72 52 A22 22 0 0 0 50 30. Both quarter-arcs terminate at (50,30); the round linecaps fuse into a single apex point — the keystone, expressed as geometry rather than as a shape.
  • No accent color — the one variation with no gold-bright at all. Its single defining detail is the meeting point itself; adding a colored dot there would be a second idea.
  • Stroke discipline — 3.5 at 128px, 5 at 32px. Heavier than any Round 1 stroke; the weight is where the confidence lives.
  • Negative space is the vault — the doorway exists only between the lines. Open at the base, closed at the crown: the circle of friends meets at one point of trust.
  • At 32px — geometry unchanged, weight raised to 5; the apex stays a crisp single point because both arcs share the same terminus.
Variation 03 Hermès register

The Framed Wordmark

The arch stops competing with the name and starts serving it. A clean arched frame — one stroke outline, one threshold line — surrounds TRÉSOR set in Playfair Display, the way Hermès sets its name inside a structural frame. The frame is architecture, the wordmark is identity, and nothing else is invited. The vault holds one treasure: the word. The É carries its gold accent, as it does everywhere in the house (§1.1).

Gold · Charcoal — 128px & 32px

Ink · Cream — 128px & 32px

Construction. Frame: M26 88 L26 46 A24 24 0 0 1 74 46 L74 88, stroke-width 2, round caps. Threshold: M20 88 L80 88 at the same weight, extending 6 units past each foot — the frame stands on its base rather than floating. Wordmark in Playfair Display 500, tracked 0.15em, centered at (50,72), set at the largest size the 48-unit portal admits with even clearance to both jambs.
  • The É accent is always gold — #E8D5A3 on dark, #9A7E4A on cream. At this scale the whole É carries the accent (the bare aigu would fall below a hairline); at production sizes above 96px only the aigu itself is gold, per §1.1.
  • Exactly three elements — frame, threshold, name. The frame takes the same geometry as Variation 02's negative space, closed into a border; the two share a family resemblance without repeating each other.
  • At 32px — the wordmark is illegible, so it retires; the frame keeps its threshold and takes the Playfair T at font-size 34 as its tenant, consistent with the monogram fallback strategy (§1.5). Below 24px, the frame alone.
  • Role note — the one variation that embeds the name, which makes it the natural lockup for welcome screens and printed invitations; the other two are better glyph marks.

Handoff · For Nasser

Where Each Mark Would Live

01 · Solid Silhouette

The hero candidate. The only mark of the three with true mass — it holds an app icon, a watermark, an avatar, a splash screen without any support. Strongest at 32px; the fill does all the work.

02 · Converging Lines

The modern glyph. Lightest on the page — suited to headers, dividers, and moments where the mark sits beside text. Its risk is proximity to generic archway marks; its defense is the single-point convergence.

03 · Framed Wordmark

The lockup. Carries the name, so it owns the welcome moment, printed invitations, and borrow certificates. Not a glyph — below 64px it falls back to frame-and-T, and below that the frame alone.

My recommendation is Variation 01. It is the direct answer to both rejections: the mass Round 1 never had, delivered in exactly two elements where Round 3 needed nine. It is the Tiffany move — a solid shape you recognize across the room, wearing a single jewel. If it is chosen, next step is cutting the clearspace and app-icon grid against the wordmark's cap height, and deciding whether 02 survives as a secondary line-weight glyph for in-app dividers.
