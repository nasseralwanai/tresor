Trésor — Vault Arch V5 · The Moderate Middle

Round 1 was too basic. Round 2 was too detailed. Round 4 was too minimalist. This is the middle — moderate architectural presence, crafted and intentional. One drawing, held constant across three treatments: a semicircular vault arch of seven voussoirs, a true wedge keystone at the crown, and a threshold line that grounds the structure.

Variation I

The Arched Doorway

Pure stroke — a confident line drawing of a bank-vault entrance. Four mortar joints articulate the arch into stones; the keystone alone carries a solid fill, marking the point where the structure locks.

Construction

  • Semicircular arch, outer radius 20 on a 100-unit grid; crown at 20 % of cap height
  • Seven voussoirs — four joint lines plus the keystone's own edges
  • Keystone wedge: 9.0 across the top, 6.2 at the seat, correct taper
  • Main line 2.2 units at 128 px; optically corrected to 3.5 at 32 px
  • Joints at 1.5 units — secondary detail, kept subordinate to the outline
  • At 32 px the keystone resolves to a diamond and joints reduce to the springing pair
Variation II

The Double Line

The same doorway with an inner intrados curve at radius 14 — a parallel line that gives the vault wall thickness. The joints now bridge the two curves, and the band of masonry becomes legible as a cross-section.

Construction

  • Intrados at radius 14 — a 6-unit band, drawn as curve only, no inner pier lines
  • Inner curve at 1.5 units: a secondary line suggesting depth, not a second outline
  • Joint lines span the full band, inner radius to outer, making the masonry read
  • Keystone seat meets the intrados; the wedge visibly locks the two curves
  • Outer structure identical to Variation I — same arch, piers, and threshold
  • At 32 px both curves hold; joints reduce to the springing pair
Variation III

The Filled Arch

The band and piers cast as solid vermeil — a gradient running bright to deep across the structure, like light falling on metal. Joints are engraved into the surface; the keystone is set a shade brighter, a stone within the gold.

Construction

  • Band filled between radii 20 and 14; piers as 6-unit solids continuous with the band
  • Vermeil gradient in structure space — bright gold at the crown-left, deep at the base-right
  • On cream the gradient starts at mid-gold for contrast against the light ground
  • Joints engraved in deep gold on charcoal; in 40 % ink on cream
  • Keystone one step brighter than the band, edged with the engraving tone
  • Same seven-voussoir count and threshold line as the stroked variations
