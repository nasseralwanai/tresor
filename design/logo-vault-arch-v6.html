Identity · Round 6 · Refinement

The Vault Arch — Refined (V6)

Variations A and B from Round 3, refined. No new ideas, no new elements — the same two built objects with tightened proportion, standardized line weight, and disciplined spacing. Three refined versions of each: six proofs, each shown in both colorways at 128px and 32px.

Two Concepts · Three Versions Each

Stone & Inlay, Tightened

Concept A stays true architectural elevation — solid masonry, radial joints, projecting keystone. Concept B stays two-tone Deco marquetry — stepped courses, concentric crown, negative-space aperture. Each version below changes exactly one thing, so the differences can be judged in isolation.

A1
Concept A · Balanced

The Architectural Vault — Balanced

The V3 structure, re-centered and re-weighted. The arc's center rises one unit to (50,57) so the elevation gains a breath of margin below the plinth; every mortar joint — radial, coursing, outline — is standardized to a single 0.85 weight so the masonry feels cut by one chisel; and the bright keystone now projects to y=15, a full ten units of crown above the extrados, so it reads unmistakably as the lock of the structure. Nothing added, nothing removed — the tightest execution of the built door.

Gold · Charcoal — 128px & 32px

Ink · Cream — 128px & 32px

Refinement. Three changes from V3-A, nothing else: arc center (50,58) → (50,57), all mortar joints 0.9 → 0.85, keystone crown y=16.5 → y=15.
  • Re-centered — the whole ring, imposts and reveal rise one unit; jambs stretch to 25.5 tall (courses re-thirded at y=69, 77.5) so they still land on the plinth at y=86. Margin above the keystone and below the shadow now balance.
  • One chisel — every charcoal/cream joint (radials, outlines, jamb coursing) is exactly 0.85. V3 was uniform at 0.9; the hair lighter weight lets the fills read more solid.
  • The lock — keystone trapezoid M41.5 15 L58.5 15 L54.8 31.5 L45.2 31.5, projecting 5 units above the extrados apex (y=20), up from 3.5 in V3: the bright stone is unambiguous even at thumbnail scale.
  • At 32px — same retirement rules as V3: steps, reveal, jamb coursing and four of six joints drop; strokes widen to 1.2–1.6 for optical weight.
A2
Concept A · Clean

The Architectural Vault — Clean

A1's exact proportions, minus the two faint threshold steps. The hypothesis: at 128px the step lines read as noise inside the dark opening, and the reveal arch alone — now carried all the way down to the plinth — communicates the interior falling away. If the depth still reads, the steps were clutter; if the opening goes flat, they earn their place back. One element removed, everything else identical.

B1
Concept B · Crisp

The Geometric Inlay — Crisp

The V3 marquetry with every seam trued. Courses butt directly against each other — shared edges at y=68 and y=78, no charcoal showing between planes, the way real inlay is set; every hairline structure line is standardized to 0.55; and the Deco keystone grows to exactly 8 × 11, one unit taller, so it breaks the crown's silhouette decisively and reads as a keystone rather than a notch. The cleanest setting of the marquetry — every plane tight against its neighbor.

Ink · Cream — 128px & 32px

Refinement. Three changes from V3-B: courses set edge-to-edge with zero gap (each course's top edge is exactly the previous course's bottom edge — y=58/68/78, verified shared coordinates), structure lines 0.6 → 0.55 throughout, keystone 8×10 at y=33 → 8×11 at y=32.

The Geometric Inlay — Wide

B1 with a broader stance. The base course extends from 68 to 72 units wide (x=14→86) and the middle course steps out to match (x=20→80), steepening the ziggurat's rake while the crown holds its position. The portal plants itself wider on the ground line without gaining any height. Same marquetry, more spread — the inlay as a low, wide vault face rather than a tall doorway.

For Review

What Each Version Tests

A1 · Balanced

The control, perfected. Re-centered arc, one joint weight, a keystone that locks. Tests nothing except whether V3-A's construction was already right — it was, and this is its tightest form.

A2 · Clean

Tests the steps. Removes the two threshold lines and lets the extended reveal arch carry all the depth. If the opening still recedes, the 128px and 32px marks converge on one rule set.

A3 · Grounded

Tests the stance. A wider, thicker plinth and a deeper shadow. Monumental if it works; squat if it doesn't. A pure footing question — everything above the plinth is A1.

B1 · Crisp

The control, trued. Butted courses, one hairline weight, a keystone that finally projects like one. Tests whether V3-B's looseness was in the seams — this is the marquetry set properly.

B2 · Deep

Tests the tones. Darkens the deep gold to #8A6E3A on charcoal only. If the inlay reads as relief instead of pattern, the dark-surface variant earns a deeper shadow tone.

B3 · Wide

Tests the proportions. Widens the two lower courses while the crown holds. Doorway versus vault face — and a candidate for horizontal lockups even if the tall version wins the icon.

Recommendation: A1 and B1 are the tightest executions and should be the reference versions going forward. Each fixes execution, not concept — the re-centered, evenly-jointed, decisively-locked masonry in A1, and the butted, single-weight, properly-keystoned marquetry in B1. A2, A3, B2 and B3 are controlled experiments that change exactly one variable each; any that clearly outperforms its control can be folded back in as a single amendment. The direction has not moved since Round 3 — only the craft has.
