Identity · Refinement Round

The Vault Arch — Two Marks, Refined

Nasser kept exactly two marks from the exploration: The Single Line and The Geometric Inlay. V7 does not redesign either — it corrects proportion, weight, and hierarchy, then offers three closely-spaced executions of each.

What changed and why. Both approved marks had the right idea and slightly loose execution. The Single Line's jambs were too tall for its arch — more tunnel than doorway — so the springing line rises and the stroke gains presence. The Inlay's keystone was timid, its crown bands too even, its aperture too narrow, and its three courses mechanically equal — each is corrected without touching the concept. Same doors. Truer proportions.

Approved Mark 01

The Single Line

One continuous stroke traces the doorway — left jamb, crown, right jamb — and a gold sill marks the threshold. The treasure is the space within, defined by absence. The refinement: the arch now rises higher on shorter jambs (springing at y=50, radius 24 — a 38:24 jamb-to-rise ratio), the stroke thickens from 3 to 3.5, and the sill extends to a deliberate overhang.

v7-1a

The Corrected Doorway

The approved mark with its geometry made true. Springing line raised from y=46 to y=50, radius widened from 22 to 24 — the apex lands at y=26 and the arch is now a perfect semicircle over its 48-unit span. More doorway, less tunnel. Nothing added. The clean baseline the other two versions build on.

Gold · Charcoal — 128px & 32px

Ink · Cream — 128px & 32px

Changed from the approved mark. Path is now M26 88 L26 50 A24 24 0 0 1 74 50 L74 88 — jambs at x=26/74, apex at (50, 26).
  • Proportion — springing raised y=46 → y=50, radius 22 → 24. Jamb-to-rise goes from 42:22 (tunnel) to 38:24, a clean 1:1.58 — the arch reads as a doorway again.
  • Weight — arch stroke 3 → 3.5 at 128px; the mark gains hero presence without losing restraint. 32px stays at 4.
  • Sill — extended x=20–80 → x=17–83, so the overhang past the jambs (9 units each side) reads as a deliberate threshold. At 32px the sill drops from 2 to 1.75 so it no longer competes with the arch.
v7-1b

The Doubled Threshold

Identical geometry to v7-1a, with one whisper added: a second sill line 3 units below the first at 40% opacity — the threshold reflected, as if the doorway stood on polished stone. The arch remains a single stroke; the echo lives only at the ground. The quietest possible way to give the mark a floor.

Ink · Cream — 128px & 32px

Changed from v7-1a. One addition only.
  • Reflection sill — second line at y=91, same length (x=17–83) and weight, at 40% opacity. Reads as a reflection, not a second rule.
  • At 32px it retires — 3 units is under 1px at favicon scale; two lines would blur into one thick smudge. The small mark falls back to v7-1a's single sill, so both versions share one favicon.
v7-1c

The Solid Base

Identical geometry to v7-1a, but the sill line becomes a thin filled rectangle — a solid plinth of gold under the drawn arch. The one line above, the one mass below: stroke meets stone. The most architectural of the three, and the only one with a filled element.

Gold · Charcoal — 128px & 32px

Ink · Cream — 128px & 32px

Changed from v7-1a. The sill line is replaced, not supplemented.
  • Plinth — filled rect x=17, y=87, 66×2, rx=1, centred on the jamb terminals at y=88 so the strokes appear to land on it. Accent gold #C9A961 on charcoal, gold-deep #9A7E4A on cream per the small-gold-on-light rule (§2.1).
  • At 32px — the plinth thickens to 2.5 for optical weight, mirroring the arch's own 3.5 → 4 bump.
  • No gradient — the fill is flat; the Vermeil gradient stays reserved for product surfaces (§2.2), not the mark.

Approved Mark 02

The Geometric Inlay

Art Deco marquetry — a stepped portal built from closed geometric planes in two golds, crowned by concentric half-disk bands and a squared keystone, with the doorway cut through it in negative space. The refinement: a keystone with real authority, a dominant outer band, a wider aperture, a taller base course, and structure lines that survive every display.

v7-Ba

The Corrected Portal

The approved mark with every proportion tuned. The keystone grows to 8×12 and projects boldly above the crown; the bright outer band widens to dominate the deep inner band; the aperture opens to a truer doorway ratio; and the base course deepens to 12 units — the taller footing every real Deco facade stands on. The clean baseline the other two versions build on.

Gold · Charcoal — 128px & 32px

Ink · Cream — 128px & 32px

Changed from the approved mark. All arcs still share one center — now (50,56), the raised springing line.
  • Keystone — 8×10 at y=33 → 8×12 at y=29. It now projects 5 units above the crown apex (y=34) instead of 3 — real Deco authority at the axis.
  • Crown hierarchy — bands rebalanced from r22/r15/r9 to r22/r14/r9: the bright outer band widens to 8 units, the deep inner narrows to 5. The outer band now clearly dominates.
  • Aperture — widened from 18 to 20 units (x=40–60), taking the doorway-to-opening ratio from 0.41 to 0.45. The r=9 crown meets the slot with a 1-unit rebate at each springing — the impost step, kept deliberate.
  • Courses — 10/10/10 → 10/10/12: the base course deepens (y=76–88) and the springing rises to y=56 to keep the stack aligned. The facade stands on a footing, not a repeat.
  • Structure lines — 0.6 → 0.75 so the engraved framework survives every display. #C9A961 on charcoal, #1a1715 on cream.
  • Ground line — moved to y=92, lengthened to x=10–90, opacity 0.55 → 0.6 on charcoal.
  • At 32px — structure lines and ground line retire, but the enlarged keystone now stays: at 12 units tall it breaks the silhouette even at favicon scale, which the old 8×10 stone could not.
v7-Bb

The Visible Joint

Identical geometry to v7-Ba, with one craft detail added: a hairline gold inlay running concentrically inside the bright outer band — the marquetry joint made visible, the way a jeweller's channel-set line follows the bezel. Invisible at arm's length, present at hand's length. Detail is not the enemy of luxury; looseness is.

Gold · Charcoal — 128px & 32px

Ink · Cream — 128px & 32px

Changed from v7-Ba. One addition only.
  • Inlay joint — a 0.5-weight arc at r=15, one unit inside the outer band's inner edge, in accent gold #C9A961. On charcoal it rides the bright band; on cream it rides the ink band — legible on both because the band is the ground, not the page.
  • At 32px it retires — a half-unit hairline is sub-pixel at favicon scale; the small mark is identical to v7-Ba's.
v7-Bc

The Lit Keystone

Identical geometry to v7-Ba, but the keystone switches from deep gold to bright gold — the one lit stone at the crown. This is the É-accent principle (§1.1) translated into masonry: a quiet mark with a single point of light, exactly where the structure locks. The wordmark keeps one gold stroke; this portal keeps one bright stone.

Gold · Charcoal — 128px & 32px

Ink · Cream — 128px & 32px

Changed from v7-Ba. One fill swap only.
  • Keystone tone — deep #9A7E4A → bright #E8D5A3 on charcoal, and #1a1715 on cream — always the same tone as the bright course family, so the crown's one projecting stone reads as the lit one.
  • Trade-off, stated plainly — v7-Ba's deep keystone is the one tonal asymmetry against the bright outer band; here the keystone instead merges tonally with the band and asserts itself purely by silhouette. Boldest at distance, subtlest up close — the inverse of Ba.
  • At 32px — the bright keystone survives; its projection is what distinguishes Bc's favicon from a plain half-disk.

For Review

Choosing Within Each Family

The Single Line — 1a · 1b · 1c

Recommendation: v7-1a. The corrected geometry is the whole argument — the mark's power is that nothing was added. 1b's reflection is beautiful in print but dies at small sizes; 1c's plinth is the strongest alternative if the mark ever needs to anchor a dark hero screen.

The Geometric Inlay — Ba · Bb · Bc

Recommendation: v7-Bc. The lit keystone gives the portal what the wordmark already has — one point of light — and it is the only version whose favicon carries its own signature. Ba is the safe baseline; Bb's joint belongs on packaging and embossing, not the primary mark.

Both families now share one discipline: a single semicircular geometry, a raised springing line, and one deliberate threshold. If Nasser wants the pair to coexist in the system — line mark for UI chrome, inlay for ceremonial surfaces — v7-1a and v7-Bc are the natural pairing. Same doorway, drawn once in wire and once in stone.
