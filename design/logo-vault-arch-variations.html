Identity Refinement

The Vault Arch — Three Variations

Nasser chose the vault arch direction. Three distinct approaches, each proofed in both colorways at hero and favicon scale.

Decision context. The original exploration (logo-concepts.html) recommended Concept C — the Provenance Seal — and flagged the vault arch as spatial, not personal: it says where, never whose. Nasser has reviewed all three and overridden that recommendation, choosing the vault arch for its architectural quiet and scalability. These three variations are designed to address the original critique while honoring his choice — each pushes the arch in a different register so he can pick the one that earns its place in the system.
Variation 01

The Single Line

The vault arch distilled to its absolute essence. One continuous stroke traces the doorway — left jamb, crown, right jamb — and a gold sill marks the threshold you are invited across. No fill, no echo, no decoration. The treasure is the space within, defined by absence. Cartier-level restraint: the confidence to do nothing but the line.

Gold · Charcoal

Ink · Cream

Construction. Single SVG path: M28 88 L28 46 A22 22 0 0 1 72 46 L72 88 — one unbroken line, stroke-width 3 at 128px, bumped to 4 at 32px for optical weight. Sill in gold-bright (dark) / gold-deep (cream), per the small-gold-on-light contrast rule (§2.1).
  • Distinction from original Concept A: the inner echo arch is removed — pure single line, no depth cue. Heavier stroke for presence.
  • Scales to 32px: silhouette holds; the arch reads as a doorway even at favicon size.
  • Risk: the quietest of the three — may read as generic archway without brand context. Best as a secondary mark, not a standalone hero.
Variation 02

The Vault Door

The arch as a substantial vault door, viewed in architectural section. Three concentric arches recede inward — frame, door face, depth — communicating the real thickness of a bank vault door. A subtle Vermeil gold gradient washes the door face. A center seam splits it, the classic vault detail. Hermès ironwork: weight, craft, the sense that something serious is sealed behind it.

Gold · Charcoal

Ink · Cream

Construction. Three nested arch paths (r=28, 20, 12 on a 100-unit grid), the middle filled with the Vermeil gradient (#E8D5A3 → #C9A961 → #9A7E4A, brand guide §2.2) at 14% opacity on dark, ink wash at 5% on cream. Center seam at 40% opacity. At 32px the inner arch and seam naturally retire — the mark simplifies to outer frame + filled door face.
  • Distinct register: the only variation with fill and depth — reads as an object with mass, not just a line drawing.
  • The gradient is the brand's Vermeil, not a new invention — ties to primary buttons and gold lit like metal (§2.2).
  • Risk: the most complex mark; at very small sizes the layers collapse to a single arch with a tinted interior. Acceptable — the fallback reads as "arch with weight."
Variation 03

The Arch & T

The vault arch becomes a personal frame. The italic-T monogram — already the brand's icon mark (§1.2) — is seated inside the arch, its stem aligned with the center axis. The gold accent aigu floats above its right shoulder, just inside the arch's crown. The arch no longer says just where; it says whose. Tiffany-level integration: two symbols becoming one without either losing itself.

Gold · Charcoal

Ink · Cream

Construction. Arch frame (stroke-width 2) cradles the Playfair Display Italic T at font-size 40, centered at x=50, y=78. The aigu — a 2.25-unit gold stroke from (58,48) to (64,40) — floats above the T's right shoulder per the monogram spec (§1.2). At 32px, stroke weights and font-size are bumped to maintain optical presence; the aigu is thickened to 2.5 so it survives favicon scale.
  • Answers the original critique: the arch now carries identity, not just architecture. The monogram we already own is given a vault setting.
  • System fit: uses the existing italic-T monogram and gold aigu verbatim — no new letterform, just a new frame around a known mark.
  • Risk: the most loaded of the three — arch + T + aigu + sill is four elements. At 32px the T must be large enough to read; below 24px fall back to the bare monogram (§1.2), consistent with the brand guide's favicon strategy.
