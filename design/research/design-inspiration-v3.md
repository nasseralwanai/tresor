## 1. Dribbble — Luxury Onboarding

### 1.1 Onboarding Luxury Experience for Ounass — Manuj G
**URL:** https://dribbble.com/shots/10881486-Onboarding-Luxury-Experience-for-Ounass
**Pattern:** Single-page onboarding — the entire welcome, value proposition, and
differentiation story happens on one screen instead of a swipeable carousel. Ounass is
the Al Tayer luxury e-commerce house in Dubai, so this is the closest regional comp
we have.
**For Trésor:** Validates our "no progress bars, each screen is a complete calm room"
rule (brand guide §6.6). We should go further than Ounass: our welcome screen already
carries the wordmark + one CTA; keep the value story to one Playfair line, not three
carousel slides. Also worth studying Ounass directly (the live app) for Arabic/English
luxury tone in our market.

### 1.2 Luxury Car App – Onboarding & Login Pages — Sk Nahid Hasan
**URL:** https://dribbble.com/shots/25443745-Luxury-Car-App-Onboarding-Login-Pages
**Pattern:** Full-bleed hero photography with a dark scrim, minimal copy, login controls
floated at the bottom third. Premium feel comes from the photography doing the talking.
**For Trésor:** Matches our existing welcome screen (charcoal + photography + scrim).
Confirms the choice to keep phone input and OTP on quiet cream screens *after* the
dark photographic welcome — contrast between "vault" and "atelier" is the transition.

### 1.3 Dribbble tag pages for ongoing reference
- https://dribbble.com/tags/luxury-app
- https://dribbble.com/search/luxury-onboarding
- https://dribbble.com/tags/onboarding-ux
**Pattern (across the tag family):** The recurring luxury signals are: serif display

**For Trésor:** We already encode all four in brand-guide-v2 (§4.5 "the luxury

## 2. Dribbble — Luxury Marketplace

### 2.1 POSHHAUS — Luxury Bag Marketplace — Odama
**URL:** https://dribbble.com/shots/21416191-POSHHAUS-Luxury-Bag-Marketplace-Website
**Pattern:** "Curated collection" framing — every bag presented as handpicked, with
editorial product cards (large portrait imagery, tiny meta text) rather than dense
commerce grids.
**For Trésor:** Reinforces the 4:5 portrait card with `Nº` serial + brand + value.
POSHHAUS leans on curation language ("handpicked for quality"); our equivalent is
provenance language ("carried by Layla, March 2026") — more personal, fits a private
circle better than a marketplace.

### 2.2 UNXD — Digital Luxury & Culture NFT Marketplace — Exo Ape
**URL:** https://dribbble.com/shots/16629248-UNXD-Digital-Luxury-and-Culture-NFT-Marketplace
**Pattern:** Authenticated-ownership framing: exclusive drops, auction mechanics,
certificates of digital authenticity. Dark ground, hairline rules, serial numbering.
**For Trésor:** The strongest conceptual comp for the Provenance Ledger idea — UNXD
proves "ownership + authenticity + history" can be the *whole* visual identity. Their
use of serials and certificate-like layouts maps directly to our `Nº 007` device and
the borrow-history-as-provenance ledger. Steal the confidence, not the crypto.

### 2.3 Marketplace tag family
- https://dribbble.com/tags/luxury-ecommerce
- https://dribbble.com/tags/marketplace-app
- https://dribbble.com/tags/luxury-shopping
**Pattern:** Standard marketplace UI (filters, price sliders, seller ratings) reads
mid-market the moment it appears. The luxury examples strip chrome: no ratings, no
badges, no discount tags.
**For Trésor:** We have no prices-as-commerce, no ratings, no filters beyond category
— keep it that way. Status dots + small caps are the only "system" UI we show.

## 3. Mobbin — Shopping Apps & Tab Bar Patterns

### 3.1 Shop / Storefront screen patterns
**URL:** https://mobbin.com/explore/mobile/screens/shop-storefront
**Pattern:** Mobbin's storefront gallery (400k+ real app screens) shows the premium
storefronts (Net-a-Porter, SSENSE, Farfetch class) sharing: edge-to-edge imagery,
single-column editorial modules interleaved with 2-up grids, and top navigation kept
to a wordmark + 1–2 glyphs.
**For Trésor:** Our La Collection grid follows this. Consider one full-width editorial
"featured piece" module at the top of the collection tab — the 2-up grid alone can
feel like inventory; one hero plate makes it a gallery.

### 3.2 Tab Bar UI element gallery + glossary
**URLs:** https://mobbin.com/explore/mobile/ui-elements/tab-bar ·
https://mobbin.com/glossary/tab-bar
**Pattern:** From Mobbin's study of 3,200+ tab bars: ≤5 destinations is the norm
(6 is the tolerated ceiling — Sephora, Duolingo); labels should stay unless icons are
universally understood (home/profile/settings class); active state is usually a color
shift, occasionally a pip or filled glyph.
**For Trésor:** Our 5-tab bar (Home, Collection, Add, Circle, Profile) sits exactly at
the recommended max. Keep the 11px small-caps labels — "Ledger" and "Cercle" are not
universally understood icons. Our gold pip + line-icon active state is rarer than the
filled-icon convention, which is good: it reads restrained where filled icons read
consumer. No change needed; pattern validated.

### 3.3 Top navigation glossary
**URL:** https://mobbin.com/glossary/top-navigation-bar
**Pattern:** Luxury apps favor centered wordmarks or plain titles with at most two
trailing glyphs; hamburger menus have almost vanished from top-tier shopping apps.
**For Trésor:** Matches our kicker → H1 screen-title pattern. Keep glyph count at two
(search, notifications) max on any header.

## 4. Behance — Luxury Brand Identity

### 4.1 Luxury brand identity search family
- https://www.behance.net/search/projects/luxury%20brand%20identity
- https://www.behance.net/search/projects/luxury%20logos
- https://www.behance.net/search/projects/luxury-brand-guidelines
**Standout projects surfaced:** AURELIA (fine jewelry e-comm identity, 26k views),
NOARÉ (luxury fashion branding + web, 16.8k views), AUREXIA (jewelry identity),
THE WHITE LOTUS retreat branding (24.9k views), HOOMIA (luxury logo system).
**Pattern (consistent across the set):** (a) high-contrast serif wordmarks with wide
tracking; (b) one metallic accent color against cream/charcoal — never two;
(c) monogram + wordmark + seal as a three-part logo system where the seal version is
used for stamps, wax-seal moments, and packaging; (d) hairline geometric emblems
rather than filled marks.
**For Trésor:** Directly informed the three logo concepts in
`design/logo-concepts.html`: the recurring vocabulary of arch, monogram, and circular
seal *is* the current luxury identity canon. Note the three-part system insight: we
don't have to pick one mark forever — wordmark (done, v2 brand guide), monogram
(done, italic T), and a seal/emblem (this iteration) can coexist with defined roles.

### 4.2 Fashion app UI search family
- https://www.behance.net/search/projects/fashion%20app%20ui
- https://www.behance.net/search/projects/fashion%20ecommerce%20app
**Standout projects surfaced:** "Vault — eCommerce App UI/UX Case Study" (name comp!),
Trendily case study (14.2k views), Clothing Store App UI Kit (31.8k views).
**Pattern:** The full case studies consistently show light-ground catalogue UIs with
a single dark "membership" or "exclusive" moment. Weakest examples fill every screen
with cards; strongest reserve one screen per session for pure photography.
**For Trésor:** Keep the item detail hero unboxed (image bleeding to the card edge,
22px radius) as the one "pure photography" moment. The "Vault" case study is worth a
browser visit for how it treats security/ownership cues in a shopping context.

## 5. Synthesis — What v3+ Should Carry Forward

1. **Validated, no change:** 5-tab bar with labels; gold pip active state; single-page
   calm onboarding; portrait 4:5 cards; scrim-over-photo welcome.
2. **New idea to explore:** full-width editorial "featured piece" module atop La
   Collection (from Mobbin storefront patterns).
3. **Identity direction:** three-part logo system — wordmark / monogram / seal — with
   the seal reserved for provenance moments (ledger stamps, certificates of borrow,
   watermarks). See `design/logo-concepts.html`.
4. **Regional comp to keep watching:** Ounass (Al Tayer, Dubai) — the only luxury
   commerce app native to our exact market.
