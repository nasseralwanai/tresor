# Home Tab Research — Luxury Collection & Gallery Apps

*Compiled by Muaath for Trésor Home tab redesign · August 2026*

## Objective

Understand how best-in-class luxury collection, portfolio, and gallery apps present a **personal collection** so that opening the Trésor Home tab feels like opening a personal vault — not checking a spreadsheet. The brief from Nasser: *"Your Collection," not "My Inventory." Personal, warm, curated, prime luxury feel.*

---

## 1. Luxury Fashion Platforms — Personal Collections & Wishlists

### Net-a-Porter
- **Curated edit over product grid.** Net-a-Porter's strength is "a highly curated edit" — the feeling of a stylist having chosen for you, not a search result. Storytelling via styled shoots, designer bios, exclusive collections.
- **Personalization through editorial voice.** Recommendations feel like a magazine editor's picks, not an algorithm. Copy is warm and confident.
- **Consistency of experience** is treated as a luxury signal — deliberate, never cluttered.
- **Takeaway for Trésor:** The Home tab should feel curated *for this person*, using editorial language ("This week's sleeping beauty," "A piece you haven't reached for in a while") rather than utilitarian labels ("Recently Added," "Items").

### SSENSE
- **Radical minimalism.** Only two types of information: product image and product description. Neither distracts from the other. Short, precise copy — nothing extra.
- **Whitespace as luxury.** The layout itself communicates confidence. Sparse, intentional.
- **Personalization in the top-right** (account, wishlist, cart) — separated from browsing.
- **Takeaway:** Restraint. Don't fill the screen. Each section earns its place. One beautiful image > five thumbnails.

### Farfetch
- **Storytelling tools:** styled photoshoots, product descriptions, exclusive collections, designer biographies, and product recommendations all serve a brand story.
- **Takeaway:** Give each featured piece a *story* — where it came from, why it matters, when it was last worn.

## 2. Watch Collection Apps — Chrono24 & WatchBox

### Chrono24 Watch Collection
- **"Your personal showcase."** Chrono24 explicitly frames the collection as a *digital showroom* — "Track. Value. Grow." The language is ownership-pride, not inventory-management.
- **Two categories: "My Watches" (owned) and "Followed Watches" (dreamed of).** This aspirational split is powerful — the collection includes what you *want*, not just what you have.
- **Value tracking with interactive charts.** Estimated market values, daily updates, portfolio-level value. The collection appreciates visibly.
- **"Tell the story of your collection."** Upload photos, record purchase details, add notes. The app explicitly invites narrative.
- **Sharing with other enthusiasts** who share your taste — community around the collection.
- **Takeaway for Trésor:** (1) Frame as "Your Collection" / showcase, never "inventory." (2) Show collective value with a sense of growth. (3) Let each piece carry a story/note. (4) The circle = fellow enthusiasts who share your taste.

### WatchBox
- **Authentication and condition guarantees** — trust is paramount. Every piece has provenance.
- **Comprehensive detail per piece** — condition reports, servicing history, warranty.
- **Takeaway:** Each item in the collection deserves dignity — not a thumbnail dump, but a considered card.

## 3. Art Collection Apps — Artsy & Magnus

### Artsy
- **"Discover and Buy Art that Moves You."** The emotional framing is explicit — art *moves* you.
- **Personalized recommendations** based on preferences selected on first use. The home experience is shaped by taste.
- **Shop by Mood** (Calm, Bold, Romantic, Transcendent) and **Shop by Scenery** — categorization is emotional, not taxonomic.
- **"Artists to Know," "New This Week," "Most Loved"** — editorial, curated discovery modules.
- **The Art Genome Project** — recommendation engine framed as expertise, not algorithm.
- **Takeaway:** Mood-based and emotional framing. "Style of the Week" should feel like Artsy's "New This Week" — a curated pick, not a data point. Categorization can be by *mood* or *occasion*, not just "Bags / Watches."

### Magnus ("Shazam for Art")
- **Instant recognition** — take a photo, know everything. The magic moment.
- **Historic and recent prices** — the world's best art price database. Provenance and value transparency.
- **Takeaway:** The "featured piece of the day" should have that magic-recogniton quality — one beautiful piece, front and center, with its story.

## 4. Luxury Car Apps — Porsche & BMW

### My Porsche App
- **Real-time data as dashboard:** battery status, fuel level, range, mileage, tire pressure, trip information. The car is *alive* in the app.
- **Manage and track multiple vehicles** — the garage concept. Each car has identity.
- **Takeaway:** A collection summary dashboard — total pieces, total value, what's active, what's resting. Status at a glance, but warm.

### Luxury Car Marketplace UI (Dribbble patterns)
- **Personalized home screen** with brand collections and detailed vehicle specifications.
- **Booking/test-drive flow** is frictionless and premium.
- **Takeaway:** The "garage" metaphor maps to "Your Collection" — a set of prized possessions, each with identity and status.

## 5. Apple "For You" Patterns

### Apple Music "For You"
- **Taste-driven personalization.** On first launch, you tell the app what you like; it shapes everything.
- **Mixed content modules:** "New Music Mix," "Friends Are Listening To," "Heavy Rotation," "New Releases." Each module is a different *angle* on your taste.
- **Friends Activity** — social proof and discovery through your circle.
- **Editorial curation alongside algorithmic** — human editors pick featured content; the algorithm fills in.
- **Takeaway:** Multiple angled modules, each a different lens on the collection: what you own, what's lent, what's resting, what your circle is doing, what's suggested. Not one flat grid.

### Apple HIG — Tab Bars
- **Customizable tabs:** let people select frequently-used items. Default to five or fewer.
- **Takeaway:** The Home tab itself should be the richest, most personal surface — everything the user cares about, surfaced intelligently.

## 6. Synthesis — Design Principles for Trésor Home Tab

From the research, these principles emerged for the redesign:

| Principle | Source | Application to Trésor |
|---|---|---|
| **Frame as "Your Collection," never "inventory"** | Chrono24, Net-a-Porter | Headline + copy uses collection/showcase/vault language throughout |
| **Time-aware personal greeting** | Apple Music, dashboard patterns | "Good morning, Sarah" — sets the personal tone immediately |
| **Featured piece of the day** | Artsy "New This Week," Magnus | One beautiful spotlight — rotating, with a story |
| **Collection value with growth narrative** | Chrono24 portfolio charts | "AED 245,000 · +AED 12k this quarter" — the collection appreciates |
| **Emotional/mood categorization** | Artsy "Shop by Mood" | "Style of the Week," "Sleeping Beauties" — not just "Bags/Watches" |
| **Social/circle activity preview** | Apple Music Friends Activity | Who borrowed what, what's new in the circle |
| **Suggested actions as gentle nudges** | Apple Music "Heavy Rotation" logic | "You haven't added in 2 weeks," "Maya's birthday — consider lending" |
| **Restraint and whitespace** | SSENSE, Bugatti design language | Each section earns its place; generous spacing |
| **Each piece has a story** | Chrono24 notes, Farfetch designer bios | Notes, provenance, last-worn context |
| **Aspirational layer (dreamed-of)** | Chrono24 "Followed Watches" | Wishlist integration — what you want next |

## 7. Specific UI Patterns to Adopt

1. **Personal greeting header** — time-aware, first-name, with a soft sub-line ("12 pieces in your collection · 2 shared with your circle")
2. **Featured spotlight card** — full-width, one piece, editorial photo treatment, story copy ("Acquired in Paris, spring 2024")
3. **Collection summary strip** — 3-4 micro-stats in a row: total pieces, collection value, lent out, circle activity — elegant, not dashboard-y
4. **"Currently Shared" module** — items lent to circle members, with gentle return nudges (no dates, per Trésor's no-dates rule)
5. **"Recently Added to Your Collection"** — horizontal carousel, each card feels like a gallery card
6. **Style of the Week** — a curated pick with editorial framing ("This week, we're drawn to…")
7. **Circle Activity preview** — 2-3 recent social events (borrowed, returned, added)
8. **Gentle suggestions** — context-aware cards ("You haven't added anything in 2 weeks," "Maya's birthday is coming")
9. **Category shelves** — retained from Boutique Shelf but reframed as "Your Bags," "Your Watches" — possessive, personal
